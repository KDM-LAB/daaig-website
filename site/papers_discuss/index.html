    Field Details
    🎤 Speaker Dhiraj Golhar (Research scholar)
    📅 Date
    📄 Paper Title Generative Adversarial Network: Use of Non-IID Samples
    🧠 Abstract
    View Abstract The generative adversarial network (GAN) represents a useful generative model inspired by the theory of two-person zero-sum games. One may use such models in image generation, text generation, image super-resolution, adversarial attacks, defense, etc. In its naive form, the generator aims to synthesize the real data that fools the discriminator by accepting independent and identically distributed (IID) noise as input, while the discriminator improves itself to distinguish between the real training data and the fake data generated by the generator. In this paper, we proposed an approach in which the generator uses the statistical characteristics of the training data through the input noise by using non-iid samples obtained using the training data. The training data is modeled as a multivariate Gaussian distribution, and the mean vector and covariance matrix are extracted from the data, which are used to draw the non-IID samples from this distribution. After training, using the features from training data in the form of non-IID samples, the generator learns the distribution of original real data faster than the IID case and also enhances the accuracy and
    diversity of the GAN model.

    View Abstract This talk introduces the fundamental concepts of quantum computing, a rapidly evolving field poised to revolutionize information processing.     Field Details
    🎤 Speaker Vishal Kumar Yadav (Research scholar)
    📅 Date
    📄 Paper Title Introduction to Graph Signal Processing and Application
    🧠 Abstract
    View Abstract Graph Signal Processing (GSP) is an emerging field that extends classical signal processing techniques to data defined on irregular, non-Euclidean structures represented by graphs. By modeling complex relationships as graphs, where nodes represent data points and edges capture dependencies, GSP provides a powerful framework for analyzing and processing high-dimensional, interconnected data. This abstract overviews the core principles of GSP, including graph Fourier transforms, spectral graph theory, and graph filters, which enable the analysis of signals in the graph frequency domain. Key applications span diverse domains, such as social network analysis, sensor networks, image processing, and machine learning, where GSP facilitates tasks like denoising, sampling, clustering, and anomaly detection. Recent advances in GSP incorporate time-varying graphs and deep learning, addressing challenges in scalability and dynamic data. This field continues to evolve, offering robust tools for understanding and leveraging the intricate structures of modern data systems.

    🎓 Session 4 —

    Field Details
    🎤 Speaker Shivam Sonawane (Research scholar)
    📅 Date
    📄 Paper Title Opening the Black Box: Understanding the Need for Explainable AI
    🧠 Abstract
    View Abstract Artificial intelligence (AI) is now used in many areas of life and is transforming the way decisions are made. But one major problem is that many AI systems are like “black boxes”; they make decisions, but we can’t see or understand how they work. This makes it hard for people to trust them. To solve this, the field of Explainable AI (XAI) was created. XAI tries to make AI systems easier to understand and more transparent. In this talk, we will explore why XAI is important, some of the main ways it helps explain AI decisions, and discuss how it connects with other fields. The goal is to show how we can make AI systems that are not only smart but also clear, fair, and trustworthy for people to use..

    🎤 Speaker Parthiv Chatterjee (Research scholar)
    📅 Date
    📄 Paper Title Beyond Generic: A Deep Dive into Personalized Summarization Systems
    🧠 Abstract
    View Abstract In an age where information overload is the norm, personalized summarization has emerged as a powerful approach to distill content tailored to individual user preferences, behaviors, and contexts. Moving beyond generic, one-size-fits-all summaries,modern personalized summarization systems integrate user modeling, interaction history, and adaptive content selection to generate summaries that are more relevant, engaging, and context-aware. This talk presents a comprehensive overview of recent advances in personalized summarization, highlighting key architectural innovations, including sequence-based encoders, attention modulation, and LLM-based approaches. We discuss the core challenges in this domain, including user representation, evaluation metrics, and data sparsity, and review representative models that set the current state of the art Finally, we explore emerging trends and open questions that signal the future of human-centric summarization systems.

    🎓 Session 6 —

    Field Details
    🎤 Speaker Bhargav Dave (Research scholar)
    📅 Date
    📄 Paper Title SqCLIRIL: Spoken query cross-lingual information retrieval in Indian languages
    🧠 Abstract
    View Abstract This paper presents SqCLIRIL (Spoken Query Cross-lingual Information Retrieval in Indian Languages), a comprehensive benchmark designed to evaluate spoken query-based cross-lingual retrieval across five Indian languages: Hindi, Gujarati Bengali, Kannada, and English. The task encompasses monolingual and cross-lingual retrieval settings across five language pairs, incorporating spoken queries (male and female voices) and document collections in text. The primary objective is to assess retrieval effectiveness in a low-resource multi lingual setting that reflects real-world language diversity and access constraints. We investigate four retrieval architectures: (i) sparse lexicalmatching using BM25, (ii) dense semantic retrieval via bi-encoder models, (iii) hybrid ranking through Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) of sparse and dense scores, and (iv) a Large Language Model (LLM)-based pointwise fusion strategy (LPF) that integrates generative semantic alignment. Experimental evaluations on the human-translated TREC DL’19 and DL’20 query sets in the above five languages show that while dense retrieval improves substantially over traditional sparse models, fusion-based approaches—particularly LPF—consistently yield superior nDCG scores across most query- document language pairs. The results underscore the utility of generative AI in enhancing retrieval performance in multilingual, low-resource, speech-centric IR scenarios. This benchmark contributes to developing scalable, speech-first, and language-agnostic retrieval systems, with implications for inclusive information access in linguistically fragmented regions.
